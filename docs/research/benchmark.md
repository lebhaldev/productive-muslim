# NURDAY Benchmark Report: Competitor Apps, Gaps, and ASO Lessons

**How this was researched.** The proxy blocked direct fetches of play.google.com, apps.apple.com, comparitech.com and unstar.app. Every figure below comes from search-engine snippets of store listings and from third-party aggregators. I did not see any Play Store page myself. Ratings and prices change by region and over time, so check them before quoting them publicly. Anything marked **(unverified)** comes from a single weak source or a source that conflicts with another.

---

## 1. Competitor profiles (14 apps)

### A. Muslim prayer and lifestyle apps

**1. Muslim Pro (Bitsmedia)**
- **What it offers:** Prayer times, adhan, Quran with audio, memorization mode, bookmarks and highlights, widgets, Qibla, and Qalbox (a video streaming add-on). Source: US App Store listing via search ([apps.apple.com/us/app/id388389451](https://apps.apple.com/US/app/id388389451)).
- **Rating and downloads:**
  - App Store US: 4.7 from about 590K ratings (same listing).
  - Google Play: about 113M downloads and 4.0 from about 1.8M ratings, per the MixRank aggregator ([mixrank.com](https://mixrank.com/playstore/apps/com.bitsmedia.android.muslimpro)). Not checked on Play itself.
  - "150M+ downloads" appears only on Subger ([subger.com](https://subger.com/en/ie/service/muslim-pro)) **(unverified)**.
- **Pricing:** Freemium with ads. The App Store snippet shows Premium at $12.99/month or $34.99/year. Other sources say $9.99/year or $39.99/year, so treat prices as **conflicting**.
- **Praised:** Breadth of features and adhan audio.
- **Complaints:**
  - Ad load rose in 2023–24, with interstitials and ads next to Quran verses and around the adhan ([islamtics.com](https://islamtics.com/?p=21176), [fiveprayer.app](https://www.fiveprayer.app/blog/muslim-pro-alternative); both are blogs).
  - The 2020 data-broker controversy: Vice reported location data passing through X-Mode to US military contractors. Muslim Pro denied it and ended its data partnerships ([kulanpost](https://www.kulanpost.co.ke/muslims-reel-over-a-prayer-app-that-sold-user-data/), [fiveprayer.app](https://www.fiveprayer.app/blog/muslim-pro-alternative)).
  - Bloat: "heavier and slower" than the simple tool users first installed (fiveprayer.app, which is a competitor's blog).
  - A Trustpilot score of 2.2 is cited only by islamtics **(unverified)**.

**2. Pillars: Prayer Times & Qibla (The Pillars Group)**
- **What it offers:** Prayer times with several methods and Hanafi or standard Asr, notifications, a "Smart Qibla" compass with calibration, a home-screen widget, a prayer tracker, and a missed-fast log (record the reason, tick it off when made up). The roadmap lists local mosque times and more widgets ([similarweb Play page](https://www.similarweb.com/it/app/google/com.pillars.pillars/), [riwaya.co.uk](https://riwaya.co.uk/riwaya-blog/apps-every-muslim-needs-to-know-about)). A menstruation tracker with a "pause" option is mentioned only by justuseapp **(unverified)**.
- **Rating:**
  - iOS: 4.8 from 3.5K reviews ([apppricinglab](https://apppricinglab.com/app/apple/1559086853)).
  - Google Play developer page: 4.3 ([play.google.com developer page via search](https://play.google.com/store/apps/developer?id=Pillars)).
  - No download count found.
- **Pricing:** Free, with no ads.
- **Standout:** Its whole identity is privacy. It says it "avoid[s] collecting it in the first place", location "never leaves your phone", and there are no analytics. The app was born from the Muslim Pro backlash ([BuzzFeed News](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/ikrd/pillars-app), [themuslimvibe](https://themuslimvibe.com/western-muslim-culture/meet-the-founders-of-the-pillars-app-the-young-muslim-duo-behind-an-innovative-new-islamic-app)). The design is minimalist, built around the idea that "people check times before wudu".
- **Relevance:** This is Nurday's closest philosophical competitor. Nurday matches it on privacy and goes beyond it on journaling, mood, habits and daily content.

**3. Athan (IslamicFinder)**
- **What it offers:** Prayer times, a wide choice of adhan voices with per-prayer toggles, Qibla, Hijri calendar, duas, a prayer tracker, several widgets, and Quran ([fiveprayer.app](https://www.fiveprayer.app/blog/athan-app-alternative), [justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/505858403/athan-prayer-times-al-quran/reviews)).
- **Pricing:** Free with ads, an optional premium tier, and donations.
- **Praised:** Quality and variety of adhan audio.
- **Complaints:**
  - Ads: "loans, loans, and advertising right after installing" and "4 ads in less than a minute" ([unitQ scorecard](https://www.unitQ.com/unitq-scorecards/prayertimeathanquran/)).
  - Opt-out promotional notifications that are hard to turn off.
  - Adhan notifications that fail silently on Android, including for one paying user (justuseapp).
- No verified rating found.

**4. Quran.com (Quran Foundation)**
- **What it offers:** Quran reader, many translations and tafsir, audio, and the "Quran Growth Journey". That feature lets users set reading goals in minutes, pages or verse ranges, daily or over a fixed duration with automatic pace adjustment. Reading one verse a day keeps the streak going ([quran.com product update, May 2023](https://quran.com/product-updates/quran-reading-streaks)). QuranReflect is a social layer for reflections. Account sync across apps arrived in February 2026 ([quran.com](https://quran.com/product-updates/connected-quran-apps-expand-your-quran-journey)).
- **Pricing:** Free and ad-free. It is a waqf run by a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, the Android app is open source, and it offers a free developer API ([quran.com/about-us](https://explore.quran.com/about-us), [quran.com/apps](https://quran.com/apps)).
- **Ratings:** Not verified.
- **Relevance:** It sets the trust standard for sourced religious text. It may also be a sanctioned source for content Nurday bundles (check the licence first).

**5. Tarteel AI**
- **What it offers:** AI follow-along while you recite, mistake detection (missed, wrong or extra words, logged for review), a hidden-text memorization mode, goals, streaks and analytics. The developer says it does not yet correct tajweed ([apppricinglab](https://apppricinglab.com/app/apple/1391009396), [krowdbase](https://www.krowdbase.com/software/tarteel)).
- **Rating:** 4.7 from 9.4K reviews (iOS, apppricinglab).
- **Pricing:** Freemium. The free tier includes follow-along, goals and streaks, and is ad-free. IAPs run from $12.99/month to $99/year, with $19.99 and $239.99 tiers also listed. A family plan exists, and the "Alim Program" gives discounts to people in financial need (pricing **conflicts** across sources).
- **Complaints:** Accent handling, and "sometimes it stops listening". Users ask for more reciters, colour-coding of memorized verses, and bigger font options ([kimola Play report](https://kimola.com/reports/unlock-insights-tarteel-quran-memorization-app-feedback-report-google-play-en-gb-156338), justuseapp).

**6. Ayah – Quran App (iOS)**
- **What it offers:** Latest Madinah Mushaf, recitations, bookmarks and verse highlighting ([App Store via search](https://apps.apple.com/app/id706037876)).
- **Rating:** About 4.8, from 235–400 ratings depending on storefront.
- **Pricing:** Free with "Ayah Membership" IAPs at $1.99 and $12.99. The listing does not say what period they cover. AppGoblin estimates 0% ad usage ([appgoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/706037876)) **(estimate)**.
- **Standout:** Craft and polish around one focused job.

**7. Salam App: Muslim Companion (iOS)**
- **What it offers:** 13 calculation methods, madhab choice, manual corrections, Qibla compass, offline Quran with translation, audio and transliteration, tafsir, a hadith reader, a dhikr and dua module, a prayer tracker, and home and lock-screen widgets. Version 1.3.1 added custom dhikr and a configurable late-prayer reminder ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1629159763)).
- **Rating:** 5.0 from 108 ratings (a small sample).
- No Android listing found.

**8. Mawaqit**
- **What it offers:** Prayer and iqamah times taken from your own mosque's timetable, a choice of muezzin voices, pre-prayer, suhoor and iftar alerts, Qibla, Hijri calendar, favourite mosques, a v4 Quran with tajweed colouring, a TV app, mosque display screens and an Alexa skill ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1460522683), [mawaqit.net](https://mawaqit.net/)).
- **Pricing:** "100% free and without any ads, no subscription". It describes itself as a waqf, and its iOS privacy label says the developer collects no data.
- **Complaints:**
  - Missed prayer notifications.
  - No way to adjust the Hijri date ([unitQ](https://www.unitq.com/unitq-scorecards/mawaqitprayertimesmosques/)).

**9. Deen Tracker / Daily Deeds / Dawaam / Visual Muslim (small Muslim habit trackers)**
- **Deen Tracker:** Salah log and goals, Quran pages or verses log, a monthly overview with % of missed prayers, local-only data, offline ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6449619738)).
- **Daily Deeds:** A minimalist tracker for the 5 prayers plus optional acts (Quran, tasbih, fasting). Data stays local, and streaks survive breaks ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6747918880)).
- **Dawaam:** Small accountability groups ([mergeek](https://mergeek.com/latest/K5lYgAMYg1JA1wx2)).
- **Visual Muslim:** Fasting, sadaqah, Asma ul-Husna, and savings goals such as Umrah ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6740770609)).
- **Ratings:** Mostly too few to display.
- **Takeaway:** The niche exists but has no breakout winner.

**10. Ramadan trackers (Ramadan & Quran Tracker, Ramadan Checklist, Ramadan & Fast Tracker)**
- **What they offer:**
  - Fast log, khatm progress by juz, and taraweeh/tahajjud log ([App Store id6758881752](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6758881752)).
  - Missed-fast make-up counter and Ramadan duas. Ramadan Checklist shows 4.6 from 39 ratings ([App Store id1507752850](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1507752850)).
  - Year-end report ([mergeek](https://mergeek.com/latest/jv07EWYDza6AkPRl)).
- **Takeaway:** Ramadan is a seasonal acquisition window. The dedicated apps here are small.

**11. Hisn al-Muslim (certified) / Hisn Elmoslem (F-Droid, open source)**
- **What they offer:** Morning, evening and sleep adhkar with smart alerts, a tasbih/istighfar counter, audio, explanations, favourites, and custom dhikr. The certified app is endorsed by the book's author, Sa'id al-Qahtani, and comes in 20 languages ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1439791907)). The F-Droid version is ad-free and open source ([f-droid](https://f-droid.org/en/packages/com.hassaneltantawy.hisnelmoslem)).
- **Takeaway:** Morning and evening adhkar is a staple daily ritual. It is the "daily companion" feature Nurday most obviously lacks.

### B. General calm habit and journal apps

**12. Daylio**
- **What it offers:** A 5-level mood log in two taps, plus activity icons, mood–activity correlations, "Year in Pixels", goals and streaks, optional notes, photos and voice, PIN lock, local storage, and Google Drive backup on Android ([intuitionlabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/software/telepsychiatry-digital-mental-health/mood-tracking-and-journaling/daylio), [Daylio FAQ](https://daylio.net/faq/wp-json/wp/v2/docs/5901)).
- **Rating:** 4.8 from 58.5K reviews (iOS). Play figures not found.
- **Pricing:** Subscription at about $4.99/month or $35.99/year. It removes ads on Android (sources **conflict**: the psychiatry.org review describes a one-time purchase, [psychiatry.org](https://psychiatry.org/psychiatrists/practice/mental-health-apps/evaluations/daylio-evaluation)).
- **Complaints:** Auto-backup locked behind a subscription, and surprise charges ([marlvel.ai aggregator](https://marlvel.ai/apps/daylio-journal-mood-tracker-1/reviews)) **(AI-summarised source)**. An older review complains of manual-only backup with a weekly reminder.
- **Relevance:** Nurday's mood picker already copies Daylio's 5-level model. What Nurday lacks is Daylio's correlation insight.

**13. Finch**
- **What it offers:** A virtual pet that grows as you complete self-care goals, plus breathing exercises, journaling prompts and "SOS" tools.
- **Complaints:**
  - Android pricing reportedly far above iOS ($69.99 vs $14.99/year, [autonomous.ai](https://www.autonomous.ai/en-CA/ourblog/finch-self-care-app-review-full-breakdown)) **(conflicting)**.
  - Lost progress, with users asking for an online save option (justuseapp).
- **Takeaway:** Gentle, non-punishing encouragement drives retention. Data loss and unfair pricing drive anger.

**14. Habitify / Streaks / Loop Habit Tracker / Stoic**
- **Habitify:** About 4.4 from 28K+ reviews on Play (aggregator, **unverified**). The free tier is limited to about 3 habits. Pricing is $4.99–5.99/month or $34.99–35.99/year, $64.99 lifetime. It offers detailed stats, smart reminders and a privacy lock ([crm.org](https://crm.org/news/habitify-review), [habitify blog](https://habitify.me/blog/apps-for-habit-tracking)).
- **Streaks:** Apple Design Award winner, $5.99 one-time. Up to 24 tasks, "negative" habits to break, timed tasks, Health integration, widgets, 78 themes and 600+ icons ([appfollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/streaks/963034692?country=bb)).
- **Loop:** GPLv3, ad-free, F-Droid. It uses a habit-strength score (a missed day weakens the score instead of zeroing a streak), flexible schedules ("3×/week"), and actionable reminders that let you check, dismiss or snooze from the notification. AlternativeTo shows 4.9 from 10 reviews ([f-droid](https://f-droid.org/ko/packages/org.isoron.uhabits/), [alternativeto](https://alternativeto.net/software/uhabits/about)).
- **Stoic:** Structured morning-prepare and evening-reflect journaling with prompts, voice and photo entries, and mood tracking. Rated about 4.8 from 4.8K reviews (UK, appfollow). Lifetime and AI tiers reach $199.99 ([appfollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/journal-habit-tracker-stoic/1312926037?country=gb)).

## 2. Cross-cutting review themes

| Theme | Evidence | What it means for Nurday |
|---|---|---|
| Ads beside sacred content are the top complaint | Muslim Pro, Athan (islamtics, unitQ, fiveprayer) | "No ads, ever" is a real differentiator. Say it in the short description. |
| Privacy distrust lingers after 2020 | Vice/X-Mode coverage; Pillars and Mawaqit market zero data collection | Nurday's "no account, no server, no analytics" should be the headline, backed by a clean Data safety form. |
| Adhan/prayer notifications fail on Android | Athan, Mawaqit, Quran Majeed's support pages blaming Doze and OEM battery killers ([pakdata.com](https://pakdata.com/fix_adhan_notification)) | **Biggest V1 risk.** Test exact alarms, add a battery-optimization helper screen, and a "test notification" button. |
| Sacred content behind paywalls | 2026 roundup (fiveprayer/unstar; unverified details) | Free with everything included is a selling point. |
| Bloat | Muslim Pro reviews | The calm, focused scope is a feature. Resist adding a Quran reader or video. |
| Data loss / backup paywalls | Finch, Daylio | Nurday's free local plus Drive backup is a strength. Add a visible "last backup" status and auto-backup. |
| Times don't match the local mosque | Roundups; Mawaqit's whole model | Per-prayer minute offsets (Salam has "manual correction"). |
| AI religious answers are distrusted | Academic and community critiques ([New Age Islam](https://www.newageislam.com/islamterrorism-jihad/new-age-islam-correspondent/artificial-intelligence-islam-road-militancy-how-generative-technologies-exacerbate-extremism/d/136201), [ijie.um.edu.my](https://ijie.um.edu.my/index.php/RIS/article/view/53308)) | The "never generated, only verbatim from named sources" rule is marketable. |

## 3. (a) Features most leaders have that Nurday lacks

1. **Home-screen widgets** for the next prayer with countdown, today's prayer times, and ayah of the day. Pillars, Athan, Muslim Pro, Salam, Streaks and Daylio all have them; it is the most common gap.
2. **Prayer tracker** (did I pray Fajr, etc.). Pillars, Athan, Salam, Deen Tracker and Daily Deeds have it. Nurday could ship it as a built-in habit set.
3. **Visual Qibla compass** using the magnetometer with a calibration hint. Pillars, Salam and Mawaqit have one; Nurday shows only a text bearing.
4. **Adhan audio choice and per-prayer sound toggles.** Athan and Mawaqit lead on this. Nurday has notifications but no adhan voices (verify).
5. **Manual per-prayer minute adjustment.** Salam has it, and mosque mismatch is a common complaint.
6. **Morning and evening adhkar with a counter.** Hisn al-Muslim, Salam and the Azkar apps have it.
7. **Missed fasts / qada log.** Pillars, Ramadan Checklist and the Memorize Quran app have it.
8. **Ramadan mode:** suhoor and iftar alerts, a fast log, and khatm progress (Mawaqit, the Ramadan trackers).
9. **Localization.** At minimum an Arabic UI with RTL. Leaders ship many languages (Hisn al-Muslim: 20).
10. **Hijri date adjustment of ±1–2 days.** Mawaqit users complain it is missing.

## 4. (b) Unique features worth borrowing (that fit Nurday's rules)

- **Loop's habit-strength score and flexible schedules** ("3×/week", "every Friday" for Surah al-Kahf, "Mondays and Thursdays" for sunnah fasts). These are less punishing than streaks and fit the calm tone.
- **Actionable notifications** that let you mark a habit or prayer done from the notification (Loop).
- **Daylio's Year in Pixels and mood ↔ habit correlation** ("on days you prayed Fajr on time your mood averaged…"). Computed on device, so it stays private.
- **Stoic's morning-intention and evening-reflection structure.** This maps naturally onto Fajr and Isha. Prompts must be neutral and not religious paraphrase.
- **Quran.com's flexible reading goal**: pages, minutes or verse range over N days with automatic pace adjustment, and "one verse keeps the streak". Nurday could log reading without hosting a reader, with a deep link to Quran.com.
- **Pillars' fasting make-up log** with a reason and a tick-off when made up.
- **Finch's gentle, non-punishing encouragement**, but no pet, so it stays calm.
- **Streaks' "negative habits"** (for example "no social media after Isha").
- **Tarteel's equity pricing** (the Alim Program). Not needed while Nurday is free, but it shows how much the audience cares about fairness.

## 5. (c) Gaps and opportunities no competitor fills well

1. **A private, Muslim-centred daily journal and mood companion.** Muslim apps track rituals; Daylio and Stoic track the inner life. None of the apps found combines both locally with verbatim-sourced content. That is Nurday's positioning, and nobody owns it.
2. **Provenance-first daily content.** Few competitors show the source, reference and tafsir name for every verse or hadith. Nurday can make "every word cited" visible, for example with a "Sources" screen listing editions and licences.
3. **Calm, ad-free, privacy-proof and feature-complete for daily life.** Pillars and Mawaqit are privacy-clean but focused on prayer times. Muslim Pro is complete but distrusted.
4. **Women's needs handled privately**, such as a pause or exemption days that keep streaks intact and making up missed fasts. Pillars reportedly has a "pause" option **(unverified)**. Most habit trackers break the streak. A "streak-safe pause" that stores nothing sensitive is a gentle differentiator.
5. **Notification reliability as a feature.** A guided "make sure prayer alerts work on your phone" screen with OEM-specific hints (Samsung, Xiaomi, Huawei). Every competitor gets complaints here.
6. **Offline-first, minimal permissions.** Allow manual city entry instead of GPS; most prayer apps require location (Comparitech study via search snippet, page not fetched).
7. **Weekly or monthly private review** ("Reflect") that ties ibadah habits, mood and journal together. Nobody in the Muslim segment does this.

## 6. (d) Store listing and ASO lessons

- **Google Play limits:** Title ≤30 characters. Short description 80 characters (third-party sources). No emojis or special characters in the title, and no "#1", "Best", "Popular" or promo wording. The rules apply to translations too. Keyword stuffing can lead to the app not being promoted ([Google Play best practices](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13393723?hl=en), [metadata policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842?hl=en), [businessofapps](https://businessofapps.com/?p=70323)).
- **Competitors stack core terms in the title:** "Prayer Times & Qibla" (Pillars), "Muslim: Prayer Times, Qibla", "Islam Pro: Quran Prayer Qibla" (Similarweb listings). Suggested title: **"Nurday: Muslim Daily Journal"** or **"Nurday – Prayer, Habits, Journal"** (check the character count). Put "prayer times" and "Qibla" in the short description, since those are the searched terms.
- **Cover spelling variants in the description:** Adhan/Athan/Azan, Salah/Salat/Namaz, Qibla/Qiblah, Dhikr/Zikr, Hijri.
- **Year-stamped Ramadan titles** ("Ramadan 2026") are common (Muslim Pro, Athan, Muslim & Quran) but go stale. Nurday should instead plan a Ramadan feature update and screenshot refresh before Ramadan 2027 (expected around mid-February 2027). Ramadan download spikes are **unverified**; I found no data.
- **Lead with trust:** Pillars' press coverage was driven entirely by privacy. Use a first screenshot or feature graphic like "No ads. No account. Your data stays on your phone." Make sure the Data safety form says "No data collected" (Drive backup goes to the user's own storage; confirm against Play's definitions).
- **Screenshot story in order:** (1) the Today screen with the next-prayer countdown and ayah, (2) ayah with Arabic, translation and tafsir source shown, (3) habits and streaks, (4) private journal with lock, (5) calendar and Reflect, (6) themes, including dark and OLED. Show the calm aesthetic, because it is the differentiator against cluttered competitors.
- **Get early reviews:** Pillars launched with community support from Twitter (BuzzFeed). A closed or open testing track plus mosque and student-society outreach can build early ratings.

## 7. Suggested pre-V1 priorities (from benchmark evidence only)

1. Notification reliability: exact alarms, a battery-optimization helper and a test button.
2. Per-prayer minute offsets and Hijri ±1 day.
3. A next-prayer home-screen widget.
4. A prayer tracker, implemented as default habits.
5. A visual Qibla compass.
6. A store listing built around privacy and calm, plus a "Sources" screen.
7. For V1.x: morning/evening adhkar from Hisn al-Muslim verbatim (with source and licence checked), flexible habit schedules, a fast make-up log, Ramadan mode, Arabic UI, and Year in Pixels with mood correlations.

**Not verified or not found:** Play ratings and installs for every app except Muslim Pro (from an aggregator) and Pillars (4.3 from the developer page); current prices (they conflict for every subscription app); the Pillars menstruation "pause"; Ramadan seasonal download data; and screenshot conversion studies.
